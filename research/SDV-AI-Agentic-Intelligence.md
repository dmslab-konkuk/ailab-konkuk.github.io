# Research on SDV Agentic AI and Deep Learning-based Mobility Intelligence

**Lead Researcher: 장성훈 (Sunghoon Jang)**

Our lab focuses on core AI technologies essential for the transition to **Software-Defined Vehicles (SDV)**, encompassing deep learning-based perception, agentic decision-making, digital twins, and reinforcement learning-driven vehicle intelligence.

In particular, the AI Lab leads the **Agentic AI specialization** within the SDV-AI convergence track, with three main pillars:

- **SDV AI Agents**
- **High-Performance Edge/Cloud Computing**
- **AI-based Mobility Digital Twins**

This vision extends into a comprehensive SDV research framework that connects in-vehicle and external data with cloud infrastructure for real-time situational understanding, enables agents to make decisions and take actions, and supports safe validation and iterative improvement through digital twin-based virtual environments.

---

## Digital Twin and Domain Adaptation Agent via CARLA Simulator

### Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Risk-Aware Autonomous Driving through Simulation-based Digital Twins and Domain Adaptation

A critical challenge in deploying AI-driven autonomous vehicles is the discrepancy between simulation environments and the real world — known as the *sim-to-real gap*. This approach addresses this by leveraging the **CARLA simulator** and **Unreal Engine** to construct high-fidelity digital twin environments that faithfully reproduce diverse road conditions, weather variations (rain, fog, night), and micro-terrain hazards (potholes, cracks, manholes, ice patches).

---

## Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Cooperative Driving and Vehicle Coordination

### Scaling Intelligent Cooperation among Multiple Autonomous Agents through Attention-based Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

As the SDV ecosystem evolves toward fleet-level intelligence — where multiple vehicles, drones, and mobility platforms must coordinate seamlessly — **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)** becomes essential for enabling cooperative decision-making at scale. Our research develops advanced MARL algorithms that allow multiple autonomous agents to learn collaborative strategies for logistics, navigation, and driving assistance tasks.
